# Related work: a dated check

*L19. Search date: 2026-11-22. This file replaces the lead list of 2026-11-19, which nobody had read.*

## Method

- **Tools.** A general web search tool (US results only) and a page fetcher. The fetcher retrieves a URL and
  returns a model-written digest of the page in answer to a question; it does not return the raw page. Two
  PDFs that the fetcher could not digest (the FoundationDB paper and the QuickCheck paper) were saved and read
  as text with `pdftotext`.
- **What "opened" means here.** Every entry in the tables below was opened at the stated URL on 2026-11-22.
  For arXiv entries that is the abstract page (`arxiv.org/abs/<id>`); where the column "Read" says *full
  text*, the HTML version was also opened and questioned. Where it says *abstract*, nothing beyond the
  abstract page was read, and nothing beyond the abstract is asserted.
- **Limits of this method.** Titles, authors and dates are as the fetcher reported them from the source page.
  A digest can be wrong; no entry was cross-checked against a second copy. Several arXiv entries show a
  submission date one or two months earlier than the month in their identifier (for example 2609.11957,
  shown as submitted 9 August 2026). Those dates are recorded as displayed and were not investigated.
- **Could not be opened.**
  - ACM Digital Library page of the QuickCheck paper (HTTP 403). The paper itself was read from a course
    archive copy of the PDF.
  - IEEE Xplore page of Jia and Harman's mutation testing survey (empty page), the dblp records of both
    papers (bot protection), and a UCL copy of the survey PDF (certificate error). Only the Crossref metadata
    record of the survey was opened. The survey's text was **not read**; it is listed for the reader's
    orientation and nothing is asserted about its content.
  - The full text of arXiv 2412.00329v3 (HTTP 404 on the HTML version); only its abstract was read.
  - A Towards AI post, "Does the Harness Move the Result or Only the Cost? An Ablation on a Local Model in
    .NET" (HTTP 403). It appeared in a search and is **not** included below.
- **Seen in search results but not opened** (and therefore not in the tables, and nothing is claimed about
  them): arXiv 2604.25850, 2605.13357, 2605.22175, 2607.06195, 2602.08146, 2610.30197, 2606.01416,
  2607.11098, 2609.32278, 2607.10582, 2611.00391, 2611.08638, 2609.35750, 2505.22583, 2411.13547,
  2509.26553, 2501.05255, 2410.03492, 2512.12066, 2603.14332, 2610.06255, 2505.20324, 2502.02412,
  2402.17988, 2502.05111, 2405.21047; the `umi-memory` crate; `anzal1/agentreplay`; the PyPI packages
  `agent-trace-replay`, `replay-agent`, `llmreplay`, `TraceOps`; a Research Square preprint (rs-11684582);
  a preprints.org manuscript (202610.1889); several blog posts on Ollama determinism and small-model tool
  calling. A later check should open these before anything is said about a gap.

"Verified?" takes three values. **yes**: the identifier resolves and the lead's title and claim match the
source. **corrected**: it resolves, but the title, venue or claim in the lead was wrong; the correction is
given. **removed**: it resolves to an unrelated work or could not be verified. Entries marked **new** were
not in the lead list and come from the search of 2026-11-22.

Project items are named as in [README.md](README.md) and [PREREGISTRATION.md](PREREGISTRATION.md).

## What this does and does not license

Each paragraph gives the narrowest description this check supports. No wording about priority is used: the
owner has not approved any, and this check, which opened about sixty sources through a summarizing fetcher
in one day, could not support it in any case.

- **Simulation testing and the mutation corpus (L1, L2, L12).** An application of deterministic simulation
  testing, a technique from FoundationDB and its successors, to the harness of a local-model code editor,
  with a scripted adversarial model, a concurrent user and a faulty environment under one seed. Testing the
  harness apart from the model against stated contracts, and scoring harness tests by mutation, are in
  arXiv 2610.04921; seeded fault schedules with invariants and shrinking for an agent are in a public blog
  post of August 2026. What this project reports is its own campaign: 41 mutants, 36 of them its own audited
  defects brought back, all detected, on its own harness. It is a case study of one codebase. Shrinking
  should be credited to the property-based testing tradition, though not to the 2000 QuickCheck paper, whose
  text does not describe it.
- **Component attribution (L13).** A replication of an existing analysis design (a 2^k factorial with exact
  Shapley values and interactions, as in arXiv 2605.05716 and the ShapleyFlow line) in a different setting:
  harness mechanisms of a code editor, models of 7B and below served locally, tasks from one repository.
  The outcome is inconclusive on a floor of zero successes, so the project reports a design and a null
  result, not a ranking of components. Harness-component studies for coding agents exist (arXiv 2609.20804).
- **Private benchmark (L10).** An application of task mining from repository history (SWE-bench and its
  live successors; `codeprobe` for one's own private repositories) with one added rule: every task's commit
  is later than the publication date of every installed model. 120 tasks from one repository. Public
  benchmarks apply the same date rule at scale; `codeprobe` already mines private history. The combination
  is a setting, on one repository, and its tasks proved too hard for the pinned models.
- **Exact-or-refuse and the applier comparison (DEC-07, L14).** A measurement, on replies that small local
  models actually produced, of a trade that arXiv 2609.11957 had already described and measured on synthetic
  edits: refusing uncertain placements lowers silent misapplication and costs applicability. This project's
  numbers (exact-or-refuse places 28.7 points fewer blocks than a fuzzy comparator; 319 of that comparator's
  798 placements were of text not in the file) are the same trade measured under a different source of
  failure (model misquotes rather than file drift), with comparators written for the study. Exact-or-refuse as a design choice is not this project's idea.
- **Constrained anchors (L7).** An application of input-dependent grammar-constrained decoding (arXiv
  2305.13971; llama.cpp grammars) to the quoted anchor of a search/replace edit, inside an editor's agent
  loop. Constraining an edit's reference to a copy of the input is in Copy-as-Decode (withdrawn preprint),
  which uses line ranges and single documents. H-4 is inconclusive; the project reports an opt-in mechanism
  and its measured latency, not a demonstrated benefit.
- **Trace conformance replay (L8).** An application of record-and-replay regression testing for agents, as
  in Chronicle (arXiv 2609.20625), to this project's harness: 41 recorded runs against which a harness
  change is checked. Checking replay tests with mutants is also in Chronicle. This is engineering practice
  adopted here, with a corpus specific to this harness.
- **Hunk-level confidence (L16).** A small replication attempt of localized confidence estimation for code
  edits (arXiv 2512.24560, 2604.06723) using two training-free estimators on local models, with a
  pre-registered threshold for showing a badge. The outcome is inconclusive and no badge ships. The project
  adds a negative-leaning data point and a shipping rule tied to a threshold, nothing about method.
- **Cache-aware eviction (L17).** A measurement, on one laptop and one local runtime, of how much prompt
  re-processing three harness-level eviction policies cause over a scripted 16-turn run (block eviction
  re-processed 0.42 of what the oldest-results policy did). That context edits force a re-prefill and that
  eviction should respect the cache are established in arXiv 2610.28577 and related work; deterministic
  eviction policies are in arXiv 2606.11213. This is those ideas applied where the harness cannot change the
  runtime, with numbers for one configuration. arXiv 2611.10315 is a caution that fewer re-processed tokens
  need not mean proportionally less energy.
- **Trajectory determinism (L15).** A measurement at temperature 0 with a fixed seed on one machine and one
  runtime version: 160 of 160 repetitions of short (two- or three-turn) coding-agent trajectories identical
  by request and response digest, after an earlier campaign was invalidated by a harness defect that the
  digest comparison exposed. arXiv 2609.13582 compares multi-turn trajectories on the same runtime family at
  other temperatures and reports identical serial greedy completions; arXiv 2511.07585 reports exact
  repetition for a 7B model on Ollama on single tasks. This project's result is consistent with both and
  extends the measurement to whole short trajectories at temperature 0 in a coding harness. This search
  found no study of byte-identical repetition of multi-turn coding-agent trajectories at temperature 0 with
  a fixed seed on a local runtime; that is a statement about this search on this date, not about the
  literature. The practically useful finding is the harness-versus-runtime attribution, which located a
  defect in this project's own campaign engine.
- **Energy to solution (L18).** A method note and a null result: system energy read from a laptop's battery
  controller without elevated privileges, with a stated error from its once-a-minute refresh, giving energy
  per attempt for three local models. Energy against accuracy for local coding models is studied at scale in
  arXiv 2412.00329 and 2609.33918, and energy per successful goal is defined and used in arXiv 2611.10315.
  No model solved a task, so the project has no energy-per-solved-task figure to report; what remains is the
  unprivileged measurement procedure on one machine and its error bound.
