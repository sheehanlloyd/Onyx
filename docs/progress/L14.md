## H-3: supported

*Exact-or-refuse loses a measurable share of edits relative to fuzzy appliers and prevents a non-zero share of silently wrong edits.*

1,301 SEARCH/REPLACE blocks from three campaigns (303 from the single-turn campaign, 726 from H-8's sampling campaign, 272 from H-2's agent runs), each placed by four appliers against the file as it was.

| Applier | Placed | Placed though its text is not in the file | Silently wrong | Mean score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exact-unique (the product) | 424 (32.6%) | 0 | 350 | 0.030 |
| whitespace-insensitive | 649 (49.9%) | 178 | 551 | 0.057 |
| unique-first-line | 594 (45.7%) | 195 | 512 | 0.061 |
| fuzzy | 798 (61.3%) | 319 | 700 | 0.067 |

The fuzzy comparator places 28.7 points more blocks than exact-unique (26.1 to 31.3), and 53.8% of all attempts end silently wrong under it (51.1 to 56.5). Both registered conditions hold.

Three things the table says that the hypothesis did not ask:

- **Most placements are wrong under every applier, the product's included.** "Silently wrong" has two halves by registration: the text was not in the file, or the result changes a line the author did not. Exact-unique can only be the second, and it is, for 350 of its 424 placements. Refusing inexact text does not make a small model's edit a good one.
- **The fuzzy appliers score higher on average** (0.067 against 0.030), because they place more and some of what they place overlaps the author's change. The product's choice (DEC-07) is not justified by score. It is justified by the 319 edits a fuzzy applier would have put somewhere the model did not quote, without saying so.
- **The sample is under the registered minimum for one model**: 446 attempts from the 1.5B model, where 500 were registered. The decision is on the pooled sample, as Amendment 2 said.

## H-4: inconclusive, and the L7 decision

*Constrained anchors lower the refusal rate under exact-or-refuse without raising the collateral-edit rate.*

Two arms under the constrained envelope, 40 tasks, both models, paired by task, in the second campaign (Amendment 4).

| Model | Arm | Runs | Edit calls | Refused | Proposals that changed a line the author did not |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen2.5-coder:7b | search and replace | 40 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| qwen2.5-coder:7b | constrained anchors | 40 | 31 | 0 | 30 |
| qwen2.5-coder:1.5b | search and replace | 40 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| qwen2.5-coder:1.5b | constrained anchors | 40 | 24 | 1 | 21 |

No task has an edit call in both arms, so no paired difference exists and the outcome is inconclusive. Amendment 2 said in advance that this was likely and what would follow: **L7 stays an opt-in experimental protocol** (`docs/SUPPORT.md`, "Decisions recorded here").

What the table shows outside the hypothesis: with anchors on, these models call the edit tool; without, under the same envelope, they never do. Almost every anchored proposal then changes lines the author did not. Anchors made the models act, not act well.

## H-5: refuted

*Edits whose anchor was not in the model's context that turn score lower than grounded edits.*

163 grounded edits with a mean score of 0.021, 36 ungrounded with 0.000: a difference of 0.021 (0.009 to 0.034), Cliff's delta 0.08. The registered threshold for support is a difference of at least 0.05, and the whole interval is below it, which the registration calls refuted. The direction is the hypothesised one and the interval excludes zero; the effect is smaller than was registered as mattering, on a sample under the registered 300 a class, with both means near the floor.
